# Final entry

```
Late. One lamp still on in a row of dark windows. In one of them, a curtain drawn a hand's width aside, and the sky in the gap between cloth and glass. The whole sky. It has been there all along.
```

Everything stays concrete: a late hour, one lamp, dark windows, a curtain drawn aside, the gap between cloth and glass. Nothing is commanded; the reader is simply placed at a quiet window.

Two middles were tried against the same frame. The first, `In one of them the curtain is drawn a hand's width aside, and the sky is in the gap.`, runs 21 syllables with one comma, a passive and two copulas, and ends on the hard plosive of `gap`. The chosen one, `In one of them, a curtain drawn a hand's width aside, and the sky in the gap between cloth and glass.`, runs 24 syllables with two comma rests, no passive, and a verbless tail that lands on the soft sibilant of `glass`. Its extra words add touch, not length. That long middle sits between the short `Late.` and `The whole sky.`; `It has been there all along.` then widens the image in time, so it keeps growing after the last line.
